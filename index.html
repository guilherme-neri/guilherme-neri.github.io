Working papers

Outside Options and Collective Bargaining SpilloversSubmitted

with Luis de Azevedo Gomes·SSRN

This paper studies how stronger unions generate spillovers through labor market competition. We exploit a Brazilian reform that strengthened union bargaining at a subset of firms and identify competing employers from workers' job mobility networks. Directly affected firms raise wages and union-bargained amenities. Competing employers raise wages by one-quarter as much, while amenities are unchanged. We find no evidence that unions actively mediate these spillovers. Employers instead respond to competition, raising wages most where they compete for costlier-to-replace workers, and leaving workforce size and composition largely unaffected. Within firms, pay does not respond to outside-option heterogeneity, suggesting internal wage-setting constraints.

Safety Net Generosity, Cost and Targeting DynamicsDraft coming soon

This paper examines how benefit generosity in a safety net dynamically shapes policy costs and targeting. Leveraging a randomized controlled trial and rich administrative data, I track families for up to eight years and study their responses to higher monthly benefits in a means-tested cash transfer program in Brazil. I find no effect on average formal labor earnings but detect longer duration on the program, with resulting costs smaller than previous estimates for social insurance in high-income settings. Recipients of larger transfers stay longer by keeping formal earnings below the eligibility threshold and by incurring re-certification ordeals, not by misreporting information. Targeting improves with time on the program, as families that remain enrolled longer have lower consumption, income and assets. Higher generosity does not affect this dynamic selection; the evidence suggests it induces longer duration among families with low self-insurance.
